     2→
     3→**Agent:** Visual/UX Design Research Specialist
     4→**Date:** July 2025
     5→**Scope:** Make LCKED the most distinctive, premium, trustworthy password manager visually — beyond "clean & modern violet".
     6→

    10→
    11→## 0. TL;DR — The Five Bets
    12→
    13→1. **Don't ship a violet theme — ship a *materials* system.** Proton's purple succeeds because it is part of a *material* story (depth, gradient, sunset accent), not just an accent color. LCKED should build a "violet as light, not violet as paint" system using OKLCH surface lightness ramps, not flat fills.
    14→2. **Own the monospace.** Secrets deserve their own typographic register. LCKED should adopt a single premium mono (Berkeley Mono or Geist Mono) for *all* secret data — passwords, OTPs, card numbers, URIs — paired with a humanist sans (Inter or Geist Sans). This is the single highest-impact "feels different" move.
    15→3. **Calm by default, expressive on intent.** Default to Linear-style restraint (dim sidebar, softened borders, reduced iconography). Reserve motion and color for *intent moments*: copy-confirmation, TOTP refresh tick, unlock success. Calm Tech principles + Linear's "don't compete for attention you haven't earned".
    16→4. **A signature surface treatment, not a signature color.** A subtle **engineering-grid background** (Vercel's "blueprint aesthetic") behind the lock screen + a single grain-textured vault surface would give LCKED a recognizable fingerprint without leaning harder on violet.
    17→5. **Large-Type reveal as a branded interaction.** 1Password's "Show in Large Type" with character index is universally cited as underappreciated and uniquely premium. LCKED should ship its own — bigger, animated, with a character ruler and improved TOTP arc.

    74→## 2. Security/Privacy App Visual Language — Building Trust Visually
    75→
    76→### Principles from the calm-tech and quiet-security literature
    77→
    78→**Calm Technology® principles (Calm Tech Institute, https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles):**
    79→1. Require the smallest possible amount of attention.
    80→2. Inform and create calm.
    81→3. Make use of the periphery.
    82→4. Amplify the best of technology and the best of humanity.
    83→5. Communicate without speaking.
    84→6. Still work when it fails.
    85→7. The minimum tech needed to solve the problem.
    86→8. Respect social norms.
    87→
    88→**"Quiet Security" patterns (ezpa.ge, https://blog.ezpa.ge/quiet-security-subtle-ux-patterns-that-signal-safety-without-killing-trust):**
    89→- "Use Calm, Consistent Visual Language. Color: Reserve aggressive reds for actual errors or fraud warnings. For reassurance, use softer blues or…" (snippet).
    90→- The thesis: signal safety *through restraint*, not through alarm. Security apps that flash warnings constantly train users to ignore them ("alarm fatigue").
    91→
    92→**Signal — the canonical reference (typenorm analysis, https://typenorm.com/apps/signal):**
    93→> "Signal's interface argument is restraint. It looks like an ordinary chat app on purpose, because its whole thesis is that strong encryption should require no user effort and surface no jargon. The signature touches are quiet ones: disappearing-message timers set per conversation, a safety-number screen for verifying a contact's keys, and registration locks that live where most apps put nothing."
    94→
    95→Translation for LCKED: **encryption is a default, not a feature.** Don't badge "ENCRYPTED" on every screen — make it ambient (one tiny lock glyph in the status area, never animated, never red).
    96→
    97→### Color psychology for security apps
    98→- **Reserve red exclusively** for actual breaches / compromised credentials (Watchtower-style). Yellow/orange for "weak but not breached." Green only for "strong / freshly generated."
    99→- **Soft accent colors for reassurance** (Proton's sunset, Mullvad's yellow-orange accents, Signal's blue).
   100→- **Avoid pure black backgrounds** for trust surfaces — they read as "hacker terminal," not "premium vault." Use elevated dark surfaces with subtle blue tint.
   101→
   102→### Avoiding alarm fatigue
   103→- Watchtower-style risk lists should be *opt-in surface*, not a homepage counter ("you have 7 weak passwords!" is anxiety-inducing).
   104→- TOTP countdown rings should never feel frantic — use a smooth arc, not a ticking bomb.
   105→- Auto-lock countdowns should be invisible until < 30s remain, then a single calm toast.

   109→## 3. Dark Mode Craft — Premium Dark UI Best Practices
   110→
   111→Synthesised from Eleken's "Dark Mode UI Design" guide (https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/dark-mode-ui), Linear's design refresh (https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh), and Material Design guidance.
   112→
   113→### Surface hierarchy
   114→- **Avoid pure black.** Material recommends `#121212` as primary dark surface — pure black creates harsh contrast and eye fatigue (Eleken).
   115→- **Build depth via surface lightness, not shadows.** Dark shadows blend into the background and become ineffective (Eleken).
   116→- **Define 4–5 gray steps** from deepest surface to elevated component:
   117→  - Deepest bg → cards (1 step lighter) → modals/popovers (1 step lighter) → tooltips (lightest).
   118→  - Even small lightness increments create clear depth.
   119→- **Add a subtle dark blue tint** to grays for branded depth — Material, Linear, and Vercel all do this.
   120→- **Linear specifically**: refreshed from "cool, blue-ish hue" toward "warmer gray that still feels crisp, but less saturated." Warning: "Go too warm, though, and the interface risks looking muddy." (Linear blog)
   121→
   122→### Linear's two design principles for the refresh (https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)
   123→1. **"Don't compete for attention you haven't earned."** Sidebar dimmed a few notches so the main content takes precedence. Tabs made more compact, smaller icon/text sizing, rounded corners.
   124→2. **"Structure should be felt not seen."** Borders and separators were "quietly proliferating." Linear rounded edges and softened contrast to give structure without clutter. Reduced icon usage, scaled sizes down, removed unnecessary treatments like colored team icon backgrounds.
   125→
   126→### Contrast and color
   127→- **Desaturate ~20 points** in dark mode. Same palette as light mode = visual vibration (Eleken).
   128→- **Off-white at ~87% opacity** instead of pure white — softens halation around letterforms.
   129→- **Test against WCAG AA (4.5:1) AND consider APCA** (Advanced Perceptual Contrast Algorithm), which measures contrast as humans actually perceive it. (Eleken lists APCA, WebAIM, Stark as tools.)
   130→- **Halation effect** for users with astigmatism: white text on dark background glows. Solution: offer both modes; default dark during evening hours.
   131→
   132→### Typography in dark mode
   133→- **Thin and light font weights disappear** in dark mode — prefer Regular/Medium/SemiBold.
   134→- **Very bold headings can tip into feeling too heavy** — find a balanced Medium/SemiBold.
   135→- **Increase line spacing slightly** and add a touch of letter-spacing for small text to prevent letterforms blending (Eleken).
   136→
   137→### Reference: Linear's tokens (https://oh-my-design.kr/design-systems/linear.app, https://styles.refero.design/style/90ce5883-bb24-4466-93f7-801cd617b0d1)
   138→- Primary color: `#5e6ad2` (indigo)
   139→- Typography: Inter Variable + **Berkeley Mono** for code
   140→- Corner radius: `9999px` (pill-shaped emphasis elements)
   141→- Surface: `#08090a` (near-black, slightly warm)
   142→- Accent (in some themes): `#e4f222` (acid lime) — single high-contrast signature
   143→
   144→### Linear GitHub DESIGN.md (https://github.com/voltagent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/linear.app/DESIGN.md)
   145→- "Near-black product-focused marketing canvas built around `#010102` (the deepest dark surface of any tool in this collection)."
   146→
   147→### Recommendations for LCKED
   148→- Use **`oklch(0.14 0.012 280)`**-ish (~`#13111A`-ish violet-black) as deepest surface, NOT pure `#000000`. The 0.012 chroma gives the branded violet tint without making it look "purple."
   149→- Define 5 surface tokens: `bg-base`, `bg-raised`, `bg-overlay`, `bg-popover`, `bg-tooltip` — each ~3–4% lighter in L.
   150→- Text default: `oklch(0.92 0.005 280)` (off-white with violet tint), not `#FFFFFF`.
   151→- One single accent (violet) at full saturation, plus muted semantic colors (red/orange/green) desaturated ~20% from light mode.

   155→## 4. Typography for Secrets — Monospace Craft
   156→
   157→### Why monospace for secrets
   158→- Character-aligned readability when scanning/transcribing a password character-by-character.
   159→- Tabular numerals (`tnum`) prevent digit jitter in TOTP countdowns.
   160→- A slashed zero (`zero`) prevents O/0 confusion.
   161→- A premium mono is the single biggest signal that "this app takes secrets seriously."
   162→
   163→### Mono font candidates (synthesised from https://madegooddesigns.com/monospace-font, https://fontalternatives.com/compare/geist-mono-vs-jetbrains-mono, https://rockstardeveloperuniversity.com/best-coding-fonts, https://www.wwebcustomizer.com/blog/best-monospace-fonts-for-whatsapp-web-developer-setup-2026)
   164→
   165→| Font | Style | Vibe | Cost | For LCKED? |
   166→|---|---|---|---|---|
   167→| **Berkeley Mono** | Designer-favorite, restrained, slightly narrow | Premium, "if Linear uses it" feel | Paid | ★★★★★ — strongest "premium" signal |
   168→| **Geist Mono** | Compressed proportions, sharp terminals | Modern, dev-tool aesthetic | Free (Vercel) | ★★★★★ — pairs perfectly with Geist Sans, free |
   169→| **JetBrains Mono** | Taller x-height, very readable | Solid workhorse, slightly "IDE" | Free (OFL) | ★★★★ — safe, but slightly "developer tool" coded |
   170→| **Commit Mono** | Customizable, very tight | Modern, custom-feel | Free | ★★★ — strong but less recognizable |
   171→| **Monaspace** (GitHub) | Variable across 5 sub-styles | Modern, novel | Free | ★★★ — interesting but busy |
   172→| **Fira Code** | Ligatures | Old-school dev | Free | ★ — too "programmer" coded |
   173→
   174→### Recommended LCKED type system
   175→- **UI sans**: Inter (variable, 100–900) or **Geist Sans** (Vercel, https://vercel.com/font). Geist has 5 pixel shapes shipped as one variable family selected through the ELSH axis — distinctively modern.
   176→- **Brand/display**: A humanist serif or flare sans (à la ABC Arizona Flare) for the lock screen and brand moments only. **Recommendation: Fraunces** (variable, optical sizing, free) for a humanist warmth that defies tech expectations — same logic as Proton's choice of ABC Arizona.
   177→- **Mono for secrets**: **Geist Mono** (free, pairs with Geist Sans) or **Berkeley Mono** (paid, premium signal). Both avoid the "IDE" aesthetic.
   178→- **Numeric features**: enable `font-feature-settings: "tnum" 1, "zero" 1, "ss01" 1` on TOTP/password displays. `tnum` = tabular numerals (no jitter), `zero` = slashed zero, `ss01` varies by font but often enables alternate forms.
   179→
   180→### Variable font weight for hierarchy
   181→- Inter and Geist both ship as variable fonts: "single file delivers every weight from 100 to 900 with smooth interpolation" (https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/inter-geist-plus-jakarta-sans-webflow-b2b-2026).
   182→- Use weight, NOT size, for subtle hierarchy: 450 (light-ish regular) for body, 550 for emphasis, 650 for headings. This avoids the "5 different font sizes" anti-pattern.

   186→## 5. Micro-Interactions & Motion Design
   187→
   188→### Framer Motion craft (from Maxime Heckel's deep dive, https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/framer-motion-layout-animations/)
   189→
   190→**The three primitives:**
   191→1. **`layout` prop** — smoothly animates position/size when a component re-renders. Use on list items, cards, expandable rows.
   192→2. **`layout="position"`** — when only position should animate (prevents "squished content" when list items are removed). Critical for password list reordering.
   193→3. **`layoutId`** (shared layout animations) — animates between multiple instances of a component. The Vercel/Linear-style tab indicator that slides between tabs uses this. Also used for hover highlights.
   194→
   195→**Key tricks:**
   196→- **Set `borderRadius` and `boxShadow` as inline styles** when using `layout` to prevent distortion during the transition. CSS variables will NOT fix this.
   197→- **`AnimatePresence`** for exit animations — combine with `layout` for graceful list add/remove.
   198→- **`LayoutGroup`** to namespace `layoutId`s when a component is reused multiple times on a page.
   199→
   200→### Rauno Freiberg's craft reference (Devouring Details, https://devouringdetails.com)
   201→23 chapters on interaction craft. The Principles unit covers:
   202→- Inferring intent
   203→- Interaction metaphors
   204→- Ergonomic interactions
   205→- Simulating physics
   206→- Motion choreography
   207→- Responsive interfaces
   208→- Contained gestures
   209→- Drawing inspiration
   210→
   211→Rauno's mantra (from rauno.me): *"Make it fast. Make it beautiful. Make it consistent. Make it carefully. Make it timeless. Make it soulful. Make it."*
   212→
   213→### Signature motion patterns for LCKED
   214→
   215→1. **Shared-layout selection indicator** (Framer Motion `layoutId="vault-item-active"`): the active vault item's selection bar *slides* between items rather than fading. Same pattern Vercel uses on tabs.
   216→2. **List enter/exit with `layout` + `AnimatePresence`**: new items spring in from the bottom, removed items fade and shrink. Linear, Vercel, and Arc all use this.
   217→3. **Copy-confirmation micro-animation**: on copy, the icon morphs (clipboard → checkmark via shared `layoutId`), the row briefly highlights with a violet wash that fades over 600ms. Avoid bouncy spring — use a slow ease-out.
   218→4. **TOTP refresh arc**: a circular SVG progress ring that *smoothly* drains over 30s, never ticking. On rollover, the new code fades in with a 100ms opacity crossfade. No "flash."
   219→5. **The LCKED "Large Type" reveal** (homage to 1Password): clicking reveal opens a *shared-layout* transition that expands the password field into a centered modal with a character ruler beneath. Each character group (4-char chunks) is a separate `motion.span` with `layoutId` so they smoothly rearrange from inline → large display.
   220→6. **Unlock success**: the lock screen's violet glow *pulses once* (a single 1.2s ease-out breath, not infinite pulse), then the whole vault crossfades in over 400ms.
   221→7. **Auto-lock countdown**: invisible until 30s remaining, then a single thin progress bar at the top of the screen, no countdown number. Calm, not anxious.
   222→
   223→### Performance budgets
   224→- Target 60fps (16.6ms/frame). All animations should be GPU-composited: `transform` and `opacity` only, never `width/height/top/left`.
   225→- Use `will-change: transform` sparingly — only on the actively animating element, removed after.
   226→- Framer Motion's `useReducedMotion()` hook + global CSS `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` to disable all non-essential motion. Per WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 / Technique C39 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39). web.dev: "Some other ways you can adapt animations for the prefers-reduced-motion media query include removing the animation, disabling transitions" (https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion).

   230→## 6. Information Density & Whitespace
   231→
   232→### Linear's density philosophy (https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)
   233→> "The challenge was preserving that rich density of information without letting the interface feel overwhelming."
   234→
   235→Three concrete moves:
   236→1. **Reduce icon usage.** "Linear relies on [icons] to make projects, issues, initiatives, and statuses recognizable at a glance, but in some views their presence had grown excessive. The refresh reduces icon usage, scales their sizes down, and removes unnecessary visual treatments like colored team icon backgrounds."
   237→2. **Compact tabs.** "Tabs at the top of the desktop app similarly, making them more compact rather than spanning the full width of the screen, with rounded corners and smaller icon and text sizing."
   238→3. **Soften borders.** "Borders and separators help clarify the relationship between elements … While these dividing lines are intended to help users orient themselves, they had quietly proliferated across the platform."
   239→
   240→### List-item anatomy recommendations for LCKED
   241→- Row height: 56–64px desktop, 64–72px mobile (larger touch target).
   242→- Per row: 28px item-type icon (custom), 14px gap, primary label (15px/500), secondary label (12px/400 muted — username or domain), right-aligned TOTP/live-code in mono.
   243→- Hover: 8% violet wash, no border.
   244→- Active: 1px violet left-border (3px wide), subtle inner glow.
   245→- Never use underlines for selection — they read as links.
   246→
   247→### Detail panel rhythm
   248→- Section spacing: 32px between major sections, 16px between rows.
   249→- Each copyable row: label (12px uppercase tracked +0.06em) + value (15px regular, mono for secrets) + copy button (right-aligned, 32px).
   250→- Large-Type affordance: chevron next to secret fields, à la 1Password.
   251→
   252→### Density without claustrophobia
   253→- **Progressive disclosure**: advanced fields (custom fields, URLs, notes) collapse by default.
   254→- **Power-user keyboard nav**: J/K to move, Enter to open, Cmd+\ to autofill — but never visible in UI, only in command palette.
   255→- **Empty states are branding moments** — see §7.

   259→## 7. Distinctive Visual Signatures — What Makes LCKED Look Unique
   260→
   261→The goal: avoid the "shadcn/ui + violet = every other SaaS" trap. Five signature ideas LCKED could own:
   262→
   263→### Signature 1: "Vault Materials" — a violet-as-light surface system
   264→Instead of flat violet accents, treat violet as **light emanating from beneath surfaces**. Practically:
   265→- Surfaces use a near-black violet base (`oklch(0.14 0.012 280)`).
   266→- "Active" surfaces get a `box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 0 oklch(0.25 0.04 280 / 0.6)` (top edge catch-light).
   267→- Selection glow: `0 0 0 1px oklch(0.55 0.2 290 / 0.4), 0 0 20px -4px oklch(0.55 0.2 290 / 0.3)`.
   268→This mimics how Proton's icons have "depth and dimensionality" — but applied to *every surface*, not just icons.
   269→
   270→**Risk:** Inexpert implementation looks like a bloom filter. **Mitigation:** keep chroma low (0.04–0.08) and test on actual OLED displays.
   271→
   272→### Signature 2: Engineering grid background (Vercel "blueprint aesthetic")
   273→A subtle dot or line grid behind the lock screen and empty states only — NOT the whole app (that becomes tiring).
   274→- Setproduct analysis: "The Vercel aesthetic refers to a web design style characterized by subtle grid patterns (lines or dots) behind content, monospaced-influenced [typography]" (https://www.setproduct.com/blog/complete-guide-to-blueprint-grid-design).
   275→- Psychology: "technical, precise, almost like a blueprint" — builds trust for a security product.
   276→- Implementation: a single CSS `background-image` with two `radial-gradient` layers at 24px intervals, 1px dots at 6% white opacity.
   277→
   278→**Risk:** Overused in 2024–2025 dev tooling. **Mitigation:** use ONLY on lock screen + empty states + setup view, never on the main vault list.
   279→
   280→### Signature 3: Large-Type reveal with character ruler (1Password homage, evolved)
   281→Click any secret → it expands via shared layout animation into a centered Large-Type view:
   282→- Each character rendered at 48px in Geist Mono.
   283→- Beneath: a *character ruler* (1, 5, 9, 13… markers) — 1Password's underappreciated feature.
   284→- Chunk every 4 chars with a slightly wider gap.
   285→- Optional "phonic" mode for verbal sharing (alpha/bravo/charlie under each char).
   286→
   287→**Risk:** Could feel gimmicky if used by default. **Mitigation:** make it a deliberate affordance (chevron icon, keyboard shortcut `Shift+R`), not the default reveal.
   288→
   289→### Signature 4: Branded empty states
   290→The "no items yet" state is the most-seen screen for new users. Make it a *branding moment*:
   291→- A custom illustration of the LCKED diamond/keyhole mark (Proton-inspired) glowing softly.
   292→- A single sentence of copy that conveys the product philosophy ("Your secrets, encrypted on this device. Nothing leaves. Ever.")
   293→- A primary CTA ("Add your first item") and a secondary ("Import from Bitwarden / 1Password / Proton Pass").
   294→
   295→**Risk:** Generic illustrations look like a stock library. **Mitigation:** commission 1–2 custom marks, never reuse.
   296→
   297→### Signature 5: TOTP as a first-class surface
   298→Borrow from Apple Passwords (iOS 18 makes Codes a top-level section, https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-18-passwords):
   299→- A dedicated "Codes" filter chip in the vault view.
   300→- A live-updating "Codes" widget on the lock-screen-adjacent state showing your top 3 most-used OTPs.
   301→- Each TOTP card shows: service name, current 6-digit code (tabular-nums mono, 24px), and a 30s arc.
   302→
   303→**Risk:** Live OTPs on a "rest" screen could leak via screenshot. **Mitigation:** blur on blur event, hide on `visibilitychange`, mask digits until hover/focus.
   304→
   305→### On custom iconography vs Lucide
   306→- Lucide is fine for utility icons (copy, eye, chevron) — consistent, scalable, free.
   307→- BUT for **item-type icons** (login/note/card/identity) and **brand icons** (Github, Google, etc.), use a *custom set* with consistent stroke weight and a distinctive treatment (e.g., 1.5px stroke + 4px corner radius + subtle inner gradient).
   308→- 1Password's competitive audit guidelines (https://1password.com/blog/concept-first-design) emphasize consistent concept/metadata for icons across the product.

   312→## 8. Color Systems — Building a Distinctive, Accessible Violet
   313→
   314→### Why OKLCH (Evil Martians, https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)
   315→- `oklch(L C H / a)`: **L** = perceived lightness (0–1, consistent across hues), **C** = chroma (gray → saturated), **H** = hue angle (0–360).
   316→- **HSL is bad for design systems** because HSL's L is not perceptually consistent: "Adding 10% lightness will have different results for blue and purple colors. Hue changes could lead to accessibility issues from low contrast." (Evil Martians)
   317→- **OKLCH supports P3 wide-gamut** colors (30% more colors than sRGB, all modern Apple devices + OLED).
   318→- **Human-readable**: you can identify a color by looking at the numbers.
   319→- Tailwind v4 defaults to OKLCH (https://tailwindcolor.tools/blog/tailwind-css-v4-color-system-complete-guide).
   320→
   321→### Making violet feel fresh (not generic SaaS)
   322→The trap: every privacy app uses Tailwind `violet-500` (#8b5cf6). To make LCKED's violet distinctive:
   323→
   324→1. **Shift the hue slightly toward magenta** (H ≈ 295–305 instead of 280). This is the "sunset" direction Proton uses — feels warmer, less generic.
   325→2. **Lower the chroma** for surface tints (C ≈ 0.02–0.04) so the violet reads as "atmosphere," not "brand paint."
   326→3. **Reserve high chroma** (C ≈ 0.18–0.22) for ONE moment per screen: the active selection, the primary CTA, the TOTP arc. Everywhere else, violet is a whisper.
   327→4. **Add a complementary accent** (Proton's yellow-orange sunset, or a desaturated mint green for "freshly generated" feedback). One accent only, used ≤5% of any screen.
   328→5. **Avoid gradient buttons.** Linear, Vercel, Apple all use flat fills + subtle inner highlight. Gradient buttons scream "2018 SaaS."
   329→
   330→### Semantic token recommendations (LCKED)
   331→```css
   332→/* Base surfaces — perceptually uniform L ramp with violet tint */
   333→--bg-base:      oklch(0.14 0.012 280);  /* deepest, never pure black */
   334→--bg-raised:    oklch(0.18 0.014 280);  /* cards */
   335→--bg-overlay:   oklch(0.22 0.016 280);  /* modals */
   336→--bg-popover:   oklch(0.26 0.018 280);  /* tooltips, menus */
   337→--bg-tooltip:   oklch(0.30 0.020 280);  /* highest elevation */
   338→
   339→/* Text — off-white with violet tint, NOT pure white */
   340→--text-primary:   oklch(0.92 0.005 280);
   341→--text-secondary: oklch(0.68 0.010 280);
   342→--text-tertiary:  oklch(0.50 0.012 280);
   343→--text-disabled:  oklch(0.38 0.010 280);
   344→
   345→/* Accent — single violet, magenta-shifted */
   346→--accent:       oklch(0.62 0.20 295);   /* primary CTA */
   347→--accent-hover: oklch(0.66 0.22 295);
   348→--accent-soft:  oklch(0.40 0.08 295);   /* selection bg */
   349→
   350→/* Semantic — desaturated for dark mode */
   351→--danger:  oklch(0.62 0.18 25);   /* breach */
   352→--warning: oklch(0.70 0.15 75);   /* weak */
   353→--success: oklch(0.70 0.14 145);  /* strong / fresh */
   354→--info:    oklch(0.70 0.13 230);  /* tip */
   355→
   356→/* Border — felt, not seen (Linear principle) */
   357→--border-subtle: oklch(0.30 0.012 280 / 0.5);
   358→--border-default: oklch(0.34 0.014 280 / 0.7);
   359→```
   360→
   361→### Light mode
   362→Mirror the structure with inverted L values. Keep the SAME H and C for accent — OKLCH makes this trivial. Use `oklch(0.98 0.005 280)` as base, `oklch(0.45 0.20 295)` for accent.

   405→## 10. Named Design Principles for LCKED
   406→
   407→Each principle is a one-line decision rule the team can apply in code review.
   408→
   409→1. **"Calm confidence."** Default to restraint; reserve expression for intent. Security apps that shout train users to ignore them.
   410→2. **"Secrets deserve monospace."** Every password, OTP, card number, URI, key — always in a premium mono with `tnum` + `zero` features. No exceptions.
   411→3. **"Density without claustrophobia."** Use Linear-style hierarchy (dimmed sidebar, softened borders, reduced iconography) to preserve density without overwhelming.
   412→4. **"Structure should be felt, not seen."** Borders at 50% opacity. Separators as tonal shifts, not lines. (Direct from Linear.)
   413→5. **"Don't compete for attention you haven't earned."** Navigation recedes; the current task takes precedence. (Direct from Linear.)
   414→6. **"Violet as light, not violet as paint."** Surfaces have a violet *tint*, never a violet *fill*. High-chroma violet appears once per screen, max.
   415→7. **"Encryption is the default, not a feature."** No "ENCRYPTED" badges. One tiny lock glyph, ambient, never animated, never red.
   416→8. **"Motion serves intent."** Every animation answers "what is the user learning from this?" If the answer is "nothing," delete it.
   417→9. **"Respect the OS."** Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, `prefers-color-scheme`, `prefers-contrast`. Default to system theme.
   418→10. **"One signature, not five."** Pick ONE distinctive surface treatment and apply it consistently (recommendation: violet-as-light materials + engineering grid on lock screen only).

   474→## 12. Distinctive Visual Directions for LCKED (5–8 options, with rationale + risk)
   475→
   476→### Direction A: "Vault Materials" (Linear × Proton)
   477→Premium dark with violet-as-light surfaces, Geist Mono secrets, shared-layout motion. THE recommended direction.
   478→- **Rationale**: Combines Linear's restraint with Proton's warm depth. Maximizes "feels premium."
   479→- **Risk**: Could feel too restrained / "boring" to casual users.
   480→- **Mitigation**: Use Direction C (Large Type) and Direction E (TOTP widget) as expressive moments within the calm frame.
   481→
   482→### Direction B: "Engineering Vault" (Vercel × 1Password)
   483→Blueprint-grid backgrounds on every surface, character ruler reveals, watchtower-style risk color-coding.
   484→- **Rationale**: Distinctly technical/precise; builds trust for a security product.
   485→- **Risk**: Grid backgrounds tire the eye over long sessions; risk of "Vercel clone."
   486→- **Mitigation**: Restrict grid to lock + empty states only.
   487→
   488→### Direction C: "Large-Type Cinematic" (1Password homage, evolved)
   489→Make the Large-Type reveal the *hero* interaction. Every secret can explode into a 48px+ character ruler view.
   490→- **Rationale**: 1Password users universally love this feature; LCKED could *own* it for the web.
   491→- **Risk**: If overused, feels gimmicky.
   492→- **Mitigation**: Deliberate affordance (chevron + `Shift+R`), not default.
   493→
   494→### Direction D: "Sunset Accent" (Proton-inspired)
   495→Add a secondary yellow-orange accent (like Proton Pass's sunset) used ONLY for "freshly generated" feedback and the unlock success glow.
   496→- **Rationale**: Adds warmth; differentiates from generic violet-only SaaS.
   497→- **Risk**: Could clash with violet if mistuned.
   498→- **Mitigation**: Use at ≤5% of any screen; OKLCH hue 75, chroma 0.15.
   499→
   500→### Direction E: "Codes as First-Class" (Apple Passwords-inspired)
   501→TOTP codes get their own section, widget, and live-updating lock-screen surface.
   502→- **Rationale**: Apple validated this pattern in iOS 18; users love it.
   503→- **Risk**: Live OTPs on a "rest" screen could leak.
   504→- **Mitigation**: Mask until hover; blur on blur event; hide on `visibilitychange`.
   505→
   506→### Direction F: "Humanist Brand" (Proton ABC Arizona-inspired)
   507→Pair Inter/Geist with a humanist display face (Fraunces) for brand moments only — lock screen, setup, empty states.
   508→- **Rationale**: Defies tech-default sans; signals "we care about humans, not just security."
   509→- **Risk**: Fraunces is trendy; could feel dated in 3 years.
   510→- **Mitigation**: Use only on static brand surfaces, never in the active app.
   511→
   512→### Direction G: "Quiet Lock" (Calm Tech maximalist)
   513→Aggressively minimal: no badges, no counters, no risk scores on the homepage. Everything security-related is opt-in via Settings → Security Review.
   514→- **Rationale**: Calm Tech principle #1 ("smallest possible amount of attention"). Best for users with anxiety around security.
   515→- **Risk**: Power users may miss features; could feel under-featured.
   516→- **Mitigation**: A single, calm "Review" CTA in settings that surfaces all risks on demand.
   517→
   518→### Direction H: "Diamond Mark" (Proton Pass logo homage)
   519→Commission a custom LCKED diamond/keyhole mark. Use it as the lock-screen centerpiece, the empty-state illustration, and the FAVICON.
   520→- **Rationale**: Proton's diamond concept (preciousness + portal) is universally praised. LCKED could adopt a similar metaphor with its own twist.
   521→- **Risk**: Could feel derivative.
   522→- **Mitigation**: Make the LCKED mark a *faceted* diamond (not flat), with the violet light emanating from within.
   523→
   524→**Recommended combination: A + C + D + E + H** — "Vault Materials" as the base system, "Large-Type Cinematic" as the hero interaction, "Sunset Accent" for warmth, "Codes as First-Class" for daily utility, "Diamond Mark" as the brand anchor. This combination is distinctive, premium, and uniquely suited to a local-first password manager.
